**1.** (a) **True.** For integers $x \le 0$, $x^2 \ge 0 \ge x$. For $x \ge 1$, $x^2 = x \cdot x \ge x \cdot 1 = x$. (Over the reals it would be false: $x = \tfrac12$.)
(b) **False.** $\sqrt 2$ isn't an integer (it isn't even rational, as we'll prove in week 2).
(c) **True.** Given $x$, choose $y = x + 1$, which is an integer.
(d) **False.** This says one fixed $y$ equals $x + 1$ for every $x$. Take $x = 0$ and $x = 1$: we'd need $y = 1$ and $y = 2$. Same quantifiers as (c), different order, different truth value.

**2.** (a) $\forall n \in \mathbb{N}\; \exists m \in \mathbb{N}\;\; m = n + 1$.
(b) $\exists m \in \mathbb{N}\; \forall n \in \mathbb{N}\;\; m \le n$ (the witness is $m = 0$, or $1$ if your $\mathbb N$ starts at 1).
(c) $\lnot\, \exists x \in \mathbb{R}\;\; x^2 = -1$, or equivalently $\forall x \in \mathbb{R}\;\; x^2 \ne -1$.

**3.** (a) $\lnot\,\forall x\, \exists y\; x + y = 0 \equiv \exists x\; \forall y\;\; x + y \ne 0$.
(b) $\lnot\,\exists x\, \forall y\; xy = y \equiv \forall x\; \exists y\;\; xy \ne y$.
(c) Flip every quantifier, keep the restrictions $\varepsilon > 0$ and $n \ge N$ (they say which values the quantifier ranges over), and negate the inside:

$$
\exists \varepsilon > 0\;\; \forall N\;\; \exists n \ge N:\;\; |a_n| \ge \varepsilon.
$$

In words: "there's a tolerance $\varepsilon$ such that, however far out you go, some later term is still at least $\varepsilon$ away from zero."

**4.** $\exists x$ becomes an "or" over $x \in \{1,2,3\}$, and $\forall y$ an "and" over $y$:

$$
\big(1\le1 \land 1\le2 \land 1\le3\big) \lor \big(2\le1 \land 2\le2 \land 2\le3\big) \lor \big(3\le1 \land 3\le2 \land 3\le3\big).
$$

The first bracket is all true, so the statement is **true**. The witness is $x = 1$, the minimum.

**5.** Take people $a, b, c$ with $a$ loves $b$, $b$ loves $c$, $c$ loves $a$ (a love triangle). Every column has exactly one dot, so every person is loved by someone: $\forall y\,\exists x\,L(x,y)$ is true. But every row has only one dot, so nobody loves everyone: $\exists x\,\forall y\,L(x,y)$ is false. The grid has dots at $(a,b)$, $(b,c)$, $(c,a)$: a "permutation" pattern.

**6. ★** We must show the negation from 3(c): $\exists \varepsilon > 0\; \forall N\; \exists n \ge N\; |a_n| \ge \varepsilon$.

Choose $\varepsilon = 1$ (anything in $(0,1]$ works). Now let $N$ be any natural number; we must produce some $n \ge N$ with $|a_n| \ge 1$. Take $n = N$. Then $|a_N| = |(-1)^N| = 1 \ge 1$. $\blacksquare$

So **$\varepsilon = 1$** is a witness. The structure of the proof copied the structure of the formula. For $\exists \varepsilon$ we *chose* a value. For $\forall N$ we took an *arbitrary* $N$, which we're not allowed to choose. For $\exists n$ we *chose* again, and our choice could depend on $N$. This "choose / arbitrary / choose" rhythm is how every proof with quantifiers works.
